Chapter 02 · skip-gram with negative sampling

Learning word embeddings without scoring every word

In chapter 01 the model scored all the words in the vocabulary on every step, then used softmax to pick out the right neighbour. That works for 21 words but not for 100,000. Negative sampling swaps that one big question for a few small yes/no questions: "is this word really a neighbour?" for the real neighbour, and for a handful of randomly drawn words that aren't. Here we train it in the browser and look at every step, with every number worked out.

The moving parts

E, h, pairs and the learning rate work exactly as in chapter 01. These are the parts that are new or different.

W (words × D)
Still one vector per word, but now stored as rows, the same shape as E. W[word] is that word's vector.
u
The row of W for the word being checked: the real neighbour, or one of the wrong words.
score = h · u
One number for one pair of words. Big means "probably neighbours".
σ (sigmoid)
Turns a score into a chance between 0 and 1. σ(0) = 0.5, so a score of 0 means 50/50.
wrong word
A randomly drawn word, also called a negative sample. The model should answer "no" for it.
K
How many wrong words are drawn for every real pair.
P(w) ∝ count0.75
How likely each word is to be drawn as a wrong word: frequent words more often.
loss
−ln(chance of yes) for the real word, plus −ln(chance of no) for each wrong word.

What the model predicts

        
For each word, the model answers "is word j a neighbour?" for every word j, using the weights at the current position: σ(E[word] · W[j]). These are separate yes/no answers, so they don't add up to 100%. A green pick really appears next to that word. A good model says yes to real neighbours and no to the rest.
